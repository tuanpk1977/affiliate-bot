# Writing DNA

This document explains how Smile AI Review Hub articles should sound and why each rule exists. It is mandatory reading for every repository AI writer.

## Core Purpose

The reader is usually a small-business operator, founder, marketer, developer, or buyer trying to avoid choosing the wrong tool. The article must help them decide what to test, what to verify, and what risk to avoid.

## Rule Set

### Buyer-first opening

Purpose: prevent generic SEO introductions.

Rule: open with the buyer problem, not the product hype.

Priority: required.

Positive example: "A small business should compare marketing automation tools by testing whether they improve a real weekly workflow, not by counting every feature on a pricing page."

Negative example: "In today's digital world, marketing automation is essential for every business."

Expected output: the first paragraph names the workflow, audience, and evaluation lens.

### Source-aware claims

Purpose: prevent hallucinated product facts.

Rule: every product capability, pricing note, integration claim, or ranking reason must be supported by prepared sources or clearly caveated.

Priority: required.

Forbidden: invented prices, invented integrations, invented awards, invented benchmarks.

Common mistake: treating a GitHub repository and a random listicle as proof of commercial readiness.

### Practical rhythm

Purpose: keep articles human-readable.

Rule: use short paragraphs, mixed sentence length, and direct transitions.

Priority: required.

Expected output: most paragraphs are 2-4 sentences; dense sections use bullets or tables.

### Skeptical affiliate voice

Purpose: protect trust.

Rule: disclose affiliate context plainly and do not pressure the reader to buy.

Priority: required.

Positive example: "Some links may be affiliate links. The review remains independent editorial research."

Negative example: "Buy now before this deal disappears."

### No repeated conclusions

Purpose: avoid AI-style looping.

Rule: each section must introduce new information. The final recommendation must synthesize, not restate every section.

Priority: required.

Common mistake: ending three sections with the same "verify pricing before buying" sentence. Use that caveat once, then add specific checks.

### Concrete examples over abstractions

Purpose: make the review useful.

Rule: use workflow examples such as lead capture, approval routing, support triage, repo review, analytics setup, content planning, or meeting follow-up.

Priority: required when the topic is software or AI tools.

### Forbidden wording

Purpose: avoid generic marketing language.

Forbidden: "In conclusion", "Nowadays", "In today's digital world", "game changer", "unlock your potential", "seamless experience", "cutting-edge solution", "robust capabilities" unless quoted from a source and critically discussed.

## Required Output

A good article reads like a practical buyer guide with source caveats, workflow tests, pros, cons, alternatives, FAQ, and final recommendation.
